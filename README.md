# India Alcohol Industry Sustainability & Carbon Analytics

An interactive sustainability analytics and carbon accounting project applying cradle-to-grave system boundaries and IPCC Tier 2 methodology to assess greenhouse gas emissions, carbon intensity, economic contribution, and consumption patterns across India’s alcohol beverage industry between 2018 and 2030.

## Project Overview

## Project Overview

This project evaluates the environmental footprint and sustainability performance of 15 alcoholic beverage categories in India between 2018 and 2030. Using a cradle-to-grave system boundary and IPCC Tier 2 emissions estimation methodology, the analysis integrates beverage consumption data, industry revenue, population statistics, and beverage-specific emission factors to quantify greenhouse gas emissions and carbon intensity.

The project estimates Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3 emissions, carbon intensity of economic output, per-capita consumption, and per-capita emissions while examining the relationship between economic contribution and environmental impact across beverage categories. The analytical model was developed using Power BI, data modelling, and custom DAX measures to enable interactive exploration of sustainability and emissions performance trends.

## Analytical Framework

### Page 1 — Industry Structure & Environmental Burden

Key questions addressed:

- Which beverage categories dominate industry consumption, revenue, and emissions?
- Which categories contribute disproportionately to emissions relative to their economic value?
- Which beverages generate the largest environmental burden compared to their revenue contribution?
- How do consumption patterns differ from revenue generation across beverage categories?

### Page 2 — Growth Dynamics & Sustainability Trends

Key questions addressed:

- Is revenue growth keeping pace with emission growth over time?
- How has carbon intensity evolved across years?
- What role do population and consumption trends play in shaping emissions?
- Which beverage categories demonstrate the strongest decoupling between revenue growth and emission growth?
- How do long-term growth trajectories differ across beverage categories?

## Key Insight

The analysis highlights that the largest contributors to industry revenue are not necessarily the largest contributors to environmental burden. Examining revenue, emissions, and carbon intensity together provides a more complete understanding of category-level performance than any single metric in isolation.
